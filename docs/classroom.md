# Terra Vox in the classroom: a 20-minute lesson

**Two moments of the planet, one in each ear.**

For a class at a school for blind and low-vision students, or any science class. No chart and no sighted helper needed: every step works by keyboard and sound, and every caption is also spoken.

**You need:** the link <https://demondarkness010-web.github.io/terra-vox/> open once beforehand (after one visit it works offline), and one speaker or headphones. With one speaker or one earbud, use **Take turns** (press **L**) so each year plays in turn instead of one per ear.

**Learning goal:** students can say *what changed, by how much, and how sure we are*, from sound alone, and know that "no clear trend" is a real result.

---

## Before class (2 minutes)

1. Open the link and press **Put on headphones and start**. Methane plays straight away.
2. Turn on **Speak aloud** (**S**) if students are not using their own screen readers. Choose the language with **T** (English, Hindi, Spanish).
3. Press **?** to hear every shortcut once.

## Activity 1: The methane Duet (7 minutes)

**Keys:** **4** chooses Greenhouse Gases, **D** turns Duet on, **Space** plays and pauses, **R** repeats the description, **N** adds the NOAA reference voice.

1. Press **4**, then **D**. The oldest year (2003) plays in the left ear and the newest in the right.
2. Let it play for 20 seconds without saying anything.
3. Ask:
   - "Which ear sounds higher? Busier? Faster?"
   - "If higher means more methane, which year had more?"
4. Press **R**. The app says the real values in parts per billion and how confident it is ("Clear rise, high confidence").
5. Press **N**. A quiet third voice in the middle plays NOAA's ground-station record for the same two years. Ask: "Do the satellite and the ground stations go the same way?"

**Talk about:** a satellite measures about 7 km up; NOAA measures at the surface. The levels differ, but the direction agrees. Two independent instruments agreeing is how scientists build trust.

**Make it personal:** type a student's birth year in **Your birth year (optional)** on the start screen (press **?** then "Show the introduction again"). Their year plays on the left against today. If it's before 2003, the app says records start in 2003 and uses that. Nothing is stored or sent.

## Activity 2: Guess the Year (6 minutes)

**Keys:** **G** opens the game, **Space** plays the mystery year, **A** the oldest year, **B** the newest, **1**–**4** answer, **Enter** next round.

1. Press **G**. A hidden year plays.
2. Students press **A** and **B** to hear both ends of the record, then vote on 1–4.
3. Press the answer key. The app says if it was right and how far off.

**Ask:** "What clue did you use: pitch, how many notes, or speed?" Students who use more than one clue usually score better. That is a good way to talk about evidence.

## Activity 3: A "no clear trend" track (5 minutes)

**Keys:** **1** (Wildfires), **D**, **Space**, **R**.

1. Play the Wildfires Duet. It jumps from year to year; the description often says "No clear change" or "Small rise".
2. Then play the Air Quality Duet (**3**) and choose **North China** and then **Northern India** with the region buttons. One falls clearly and one rises.

**Ask:**
- "Is 'no clear change' a failure of the app, or a finding?"
- "Why might fires jump around year to year?" (Weather, drought, El Niño, land use.)
- "Why might one region's air get cleaner while another's gets dirtier?" (Laws, power plants, traffic, growing cities.)

**Talk about:** science reports what the data shows, including "we can't tell". The app's confidence word comes from a written rule, not from a feeling ([the rules](mapping-explainer.md)).

## Wrap-up (1 minute)

Each student says one sentence: "*I heard that ___ changed from ___ to ___, and I'm ___ sure because ___.*"

To share one exact comparison afterwards, press **Copy link to this Duet**. The link opens the same two years for anyone.

## Keyboard summary

| Key | Action |
|---|---|
| `Space` | Play / pause |
| `1`–`4` | Choose a theme and play |
| `D` | Duet on / off |
| `L` | Listening mode: together, take turns, left only, right only |
| `N` | NOAA reference voice (Duet) |
| `R` | Repeat the description |
| `S` | Speak aloud on / off |
| `T` | Language |
| `F` | Switch to NASA Earth Information Center visualizations |
| `G` | Guess the Year |
| `?` | All shortcuts |
